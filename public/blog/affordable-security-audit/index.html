You Don't Need a $10,000 Security Audit

By Bryon Hudson · Founder, CamLabs
June 24, 2026 · 4 min read

Ask a security firm to audit your application and you'll get a quote that makes you close the tab: thousands of dollars, sometimes tens of thousands, for a single engagement. For a funded company, that's a line item. For an indie developer or a two-person team, it's a non-starter.

So what actually happens? Most small builders do the only thing the math allows: they skip the audit and hope. That's the real status quo — not "insecure indie devs," but talented people priced out of even checking their own work.

Why Auditing Got So Expensive

Traditional security auditing is a human-time business. You're paying senior specialists to manually read your code, probe your app, and write up findings — billed by the hour, often on a retainer, usually as a one-time engagement. That model produces excellent work, and for high-stakes systems it's worth every dollar. But it's structurally expensive, and that expense is the wall most small teams hit.

What You're Actually Paying For

Strip away the invoice and a lot of what a firm does is expert pattern-matching — checking your code against well-understood classes of vulnerability: injection, hardcoded secrets, broken authentication, unsafe data handling, path traversal, and the rest. It takes real expertise to know what to look for. But "knowing what to look for" is exactly the kind of knowledge that can now be encoded into a tool and run on your own machine, in seconds, for a fraction of the cost.

Pro-Grade Auditing, Indie Price

That's the whole reason I built Sentinel AI. I'd seen what firms charge, and I didn't think genuine vulnerability testing should be a luxury reserved for companies with big budgets. Sentinel brings that caliber of analysis to a tool that:

  • Costs once, not per-engagement. A one-time lifetime license instead of a five-figure invoice or a recurring retainer.
  • Runs on every build, not once a year. The expensive part of a human audit is that you can only afford it rarely. An on-device tool you can run before every release — security as a habit, not an event.
  • Keeps your code private. It audits entirely on your machine, so you're not emailing your source to a third party to get it reviewed.

An Honest Caveat

I'm not going to tell you an automated tool replaces a seasoned human pentester for a bank or a medical device — it doesn't, and when the stakes are that high, pay for the human. But that was never the real choice for most of us. The real choice for an indie dev is Sentinel versus nothing — and "a thorough automated audit on every build" beats "I couldn't afford it, so I shipped blind" every single time.

The Bottom Line

Security shouldn't be gatekept behind a budget. You don't need a security department or a $10,000 invoice to build professional-grade, secure software — you need the right tool, run consistently, on your own terms.
