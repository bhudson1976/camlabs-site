Local AI vs. Cloud AI: What Happens to Your Code?

By Bryon Hudson · Founder, CamLabs
June 24, 2026 · 5 min read

Every developer I know is using AI now. We paste a function into a chat window, ask it to find the bug, and get an answer in seconds. It feels like magic. But there's a question almost nobody stops to ask in that moment: where did my code just go?

The answer matters more than most of us want to admit. Because "AI" isn't one thing — there's a fundamental split in where the work actually happens, and that split decides who else gets to see your code.

The Convenience Trap

Cloud AI is incredibly convenient, and that convenience is exactly what makes the trade-off easy to ignore. When you use a cloud-based AI tool — a chat assistant, a web auditor, a "paste your repo and we'll analyze it" service — your code doesn't stay on your machine. It travels across the internet to a server you don't own, gets processed by a model you can't inspect, and is handled according to a data policy you almost certainly didn't read.

In the rush to get an answer, "upload my proprietary logic to a stranger's computer" stops feeling like what it is.

What Actually Happens to Your Code

Here's the honest version of the round trip when you use a cloud AI tool:

  • It leaves your machine. Your source is transmitted off your device to a third-party server, often alongside thousands of other users' code.
  • It may be retained. Depending on the provider and your plan, your input can be logged for abuse monitoring, debugging, or — in some cases — used to help train future models.
  • It inherits someone else's risk. Once your code lives on infrastructure you don't control, it's exposed to that provider's breaches, misconfigurations, and legal obligations. Their security posture is now your security posture.

For a weekend side project, maybe none of that matters. But for proprietary business logic, client work under NDA, or anything you'd be uncomfortable seeing leak — it's a genuine exposure. And "we probably don't train on your data" is not the same promise as "your code never left your building."

The Local Alternative

There's another way to get AI assistance that flips the entire model: run the AI on your own hardware. Instead of sending your code to the model, you bring the model to your code.

The analysis happens on your machine, inside your own file system, with nothing transmitted anywhere. No upload. No retention. No third-party infrastructure sitting in the middle — because there is no third party. The intelligence comes to you and stays there.

The Bottom Line

Cloud AI isn't evil — it's a trade. You trade ownership of your code, even briefly, for convenience. For plenty of tasks that's a fine deal. But it should be a choice you make consciously, not a default you back into because the chat box was right there.

My take is simple: if your code is worth protecting, the model should come to you — not the other way around. That's where Sentinel runs, and that's where it stays.
