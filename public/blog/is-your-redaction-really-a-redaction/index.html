Is Your Redaction Really a Redaction?

By Bryon Hudson
November 14, 2026 · 5 min read

A black box over an SSN in a PDF often hides nothing: the number is still in the file, and anyone can copy it back out in seconds. Here is how to check your own files before tax season, and how to redact so it holds.

Why black boxes fail

Most PDF editors let you draw a filled rectangle over text. On screen the text looks gone, but it is still in the file underneath the shape. Anyone who selects it, copies it and pastes it somewhere else can read every word.

This catches experienced professionals, not just beginners. In January 2019, Paul Manafort’s defense team filed a response with passages blacked out this way. Reporters copied the hidden text and published it the same day. In December 2025, some of the Justice Department’s Epstein files were recovered the same way: copy the blacked-out section, paste it into another document, read it.

If it can happen in federal court filings, it can happen in a tax office’s email to a lender.

Why it matters for your office

Tax preparers count as financial institutions under the FTC Safeguards Rule, and the IRS expects every paid preparer to keep a Written Information Security Plan (WISP). A WISP spells out what client data you hold, where it lives and how you protect it.

A redaction that can be copied back out quietly undoes all of that. During the season, SSNs, bank account numbers and dates of birth leave the office every day: on W-2s, 1099s, bank statements and engagement letters, sent to clients, lenders, attorneys and other preparers. Each “redacted” file that still holds the numbers is client data you didn’t mean to share.

The 10-second test

Pick a PDF you have already redacted and sent, then:

  1. Open it and press Ctrl+A (Cmd+A on a Mac) to select everything.
  2. Copy it with Ctrl+C (Cmd+C).
  3. Paste it into a blank document or email draft.
  4. Look for the numbers you covered. If you can read them, so can anyone you sent the file to.

How to redact so it holds

  • Use a real redaction tool, not a drawing tool. Rectangles, highlights and black text color only cover words. A true redaction feature (Adobe Acrobat Pro’s Redact tool is one) deletes the text and then flattens the page.
  • Never redact in Word and save as PDF. Black highlighting in Word carries the text straight into the PDF.
  • Clear what you can’t see. Comments, form fields, metadata and earlier versions can hold the same numbers. Acrobat’s Sanitize option, or exporting a fresh flattened copy, removes them.
  • Scans need the same care. A scanned page with a box drawn over it is still two layers until it is flattened.
  • Check before you send. Run the 10-second test on the finished file every time. It takes less time than writing the email.
  • Write it into your WISP. One line covering how your office redacts and verifies documents before they leave makes the habit stick for everyone on staff.
