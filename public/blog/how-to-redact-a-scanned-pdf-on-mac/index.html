How to Redact a Scanned PDF on a Mac (Without Uploading It Anywhere)

By Bryon Hudson · Founder, CamLabs AI
October 3, 2026 · 5 min read

A client sends you a scanned bank statement, a signed form or a stack of photos turned into a PDF. Before it goes anywhere else, the SSNs, account numbers and birth dates have to come out. On a Mac you have three real options, and one trap.

The trap: shapes and highlights

Drawing a black rectangle or a thick highlight with a markup tool puts a shape on top of the page. Whatever is underneath can often still be selected, copied or uncovered by removing the shape. It looks redacted on your screen, and it isn't.

Option 1: Preview's Redact tool (free, built in)

Recent versions of Preview include a real redaction tool. Open the PDF, choose Tools > Redact (or click Markup, then the Redact button), select the text you want gone and save. Once saved, the redaction is permanent, so work on a copy (how-to from Intego).

The catch is that it is fully manual. You find every SSN, every account number and every repeat of a name yourself, page by page. That's fine for a one-page letter. On a 30-page scanned packet, it is slow, and a single miss is a disclosure.

Option 2: Adobe Acrobat Pro

Acrobat Pro can search for patterns and redact matches across a document. Redaction is a Pro feature; Acrobat Standard doesn't include it. As of mid-2026, Pro runs about $19.99 a month on an annual plan, or about $29.99 month to month (pricing summary, which plans include Redact). Check Adobe's own pricing page before you buy. For scans, you'll need to run text recognition first so the search has words to find.

Option 3: an on-device redaction app

This is the one we make at CamLabs AI, Redactly AI, so read this part knowing we're biased. Here's how it works on a Mac:

  1. Open the PDF or image. Scanned pages are read with on-device text recognition, so there's no separate OCR step.
  2. Tell it what to remove. Ask for "Redact all PII," or pick a category: SSNs, account and card numbers, phone numbers, emails, dates of birth, addresses and more. AI name detection is available too and needs a free Redactly account.
  3. Review every page. Click a black bar to undo it, or draw your own over anything it missed.
  4. Export. The exported PDF is flattened: the bars are burned into the page image and the original text layer is gone. Export, print and share stay locked until every page has finished processing.

Check the result before you send it

Whichever option you use, test the exported file, not what's on your screen:

  1. The copy test. Open the exported PDF, drag across a black bar and press Command-C. Paste into a blank note. If any hidden text appears, the redaction failed.
  2. The search test. Press Command-F and search for something you removed, like the last four of an account number. It should not be found.
  3. The page-by-page look. Zoom in on every page. Watch for numbers that wrap to the next line, names in headers and footers, and anything inside stamps or handwriting.

No automated tool is perfect, including ours. Let the tool do the searching, then make the final check yourself.

The short version

For a page or two, Preview's Redact tool is free and does the job. For long or scanned documents, use a tool that finds the items for you, and pick one that keeps the files on your Mac. Either way, run the copy test before you hit send.
