How to Redact a PDF on iPhone (and Check That It Actually Worked)

By Bryon Hudson · Founder, CamLabs
September 30, 2026 · 5 min read

You need to send a bank statement, a tax form or a court filing from your phone, and a few things on it can't be seen: an SSN, an account number, a date of birth. The obvious move is to mark them up with a black box. That is also the move that gets people in trouble.

Why a black box usually isn't redaction

Most iPhone markup tools add a shape on top of the page. The text underneath is still in the file. Anyone who receives it can often select the "hidden" text, copy it, and paste it somewhere else, or simply remove the shape.

Real redaction removes the information from the file itself. Once it's done, there is nothing under the black bar to recover. Keep that difference in mind as you pick a tool, because both kinds of tool put a black bar on your screen.

Option 1: Adobe Acrobat Pro

Acrobat's true Redact tool is part of Acrobat Pro; Acrobat Standard doesn't include it. As of mid-2026, Pro runs about $19.99 a month on an annual plan, or about $29.99 month to month (pricing summary, which plans include Redact). Check Adobe's own pricing page before you buy, since plans change.

If your office already pays for Acrobat Pro, it's a solid choice. If you only redact now and then, or mostly from your phone, it's a lot to pay for one feature.

The other route is an app built just for redaction. I built one, Redactly AI, so take this section knowing I'm biased. Here is how it works on iPhone:

  1. Bring in the document. Scan paper with the camera, or open a PDF or photo you already have.
  2. Tell it what to hide. Ask the copilot something like "Redact all PII," or pick a category such as SSNs, account numbers, phone numbers, emails, dates of birth or addresses. How to check the result before you send it

Whatever tool you use, take 30 seconds to test the exported file, not the version on your screen:

  1. The copy test. Open the exported PDF, press and hold on a black bar, and try to select the text. Paste whatever you get into Notes. If any hidden words show up, the redaction failed.
  2. The search test. Search the PDF for a word you know you hid, like the last four of an account number. It should not be found. (A flattened export has no searchable text at all, which is expected.)
  3. The page-by-page look. Zoom in on every page. Watch for a number that wraps onto the next line, a name in a header or footer, and anything in a stamp or handwritten note.

No automated tool is perfect, including mine. Use the tool to do the heavy lifting, then make the last check yourself.

The short version

A black shape is not redaction. Use Acrobat Pro if you already have it, or an on-device app if you want to do it from your phone without uploading the document anywhere. Either way, run the copy test before you hit send.
