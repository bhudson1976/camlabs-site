Manual Redaction Is Costing Law Offices Hours — Here's the AI Fix

By Bryon Hudson · Founder, CamLabs
August 5, 2026 · 5 min read

Picture the scene in almost any law office: a paralegal or associate with a 120-page deposition open in Adobe Acrobat, scrolling line by line, hunting for every name, address, and Social Security number — then dragging a black box over each one, by hand, one at a time.

It's careful work, and the people doing it are good at it. But it's also slow, mind-numbing, and — this is the part that should worry every firm — easy to get wrong in ways that don't show up until it's too late.

The Manual Redaction Grind

Redacting a document by hand is a hunt-and-cover loop repeated hundreds of times. Find the sensitive item. Mark it. Move on. Find the next one. On a long document with recurring names across every page, that's an afternoon of billable time spent on something a person shouldn't have to do manually at all. And scanned documents make it worse — a photographed or faxed page is just an image, so the tidy "Find & Redact" text search doesn't even see the words you're trying to hide.

Slow Is the Small Problem. Risky Is the Big One.

The real danger isn't the wasted hours — it's the mistakes. Manual redaction fails in two classic, career-denting ways:

  • The black box that isn't really redaction. Draw a black rectangle over text in the wrong tool and the words underneath are still there — selectable, copyable, and fully recoverable. Courts and companies have leaked SSNs, witness names, and settlement figures exactly this way, because the "redaction" was just a shape sitting on top of live text.
  • The one you missed. When a name appears 15 times across 40 pages, missing it once on page 37 is a disclosure. Human attention is no match for that kind of repetition, and the miss is invisible until opposing counsel — or a regulator — finds it.

For a law office, a single blown redaction isn't an "oops." It's a privacy breach, a potential sanction, and a very bad phone call.

What AI Changes

Here's the shift: instead of you hunting for every sensitive item, you tell the tool what to hide and let it do the hunting. "Redact all names." "Redact all SSNs." "Redact Jonathan." The AI reads the whole document — including scanned pages, via on-device OCR — finds every instance across every page, and blacks them out. The tedious part disappears, and so does the "did I miss one?" anxiety, because the machine doesn't get bored on page 37.
